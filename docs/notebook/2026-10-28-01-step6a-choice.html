Question

Which 3D cell-mechanics solver architecture can take segmented T-cell and target meshes from data/scidb_19235_masks/, apply a local active synaptic pushing load on the T-cell contact patch, represent distinct T-cell vs target cortical stiffnesses (including ~2x stiffer synaptic cortex per Jung et al. 2021), map explicitly to AFM cortical tension $T$, and solve fast enough (< 1 s per relaxation) to fit hundreds of chamber-visits?

** Verification Notice: Downstream Step 6 mechanics work is UNVERIFIED and parked at Gate G5.**

What was run

Source audit of external/SimuCell3D, external/ShElastic, external/Phase_field_Apoptosis, and timing/feasibility benchmarks documented in .agy_step6a_report.md and status/plan.json.

Commits: - 159df33 (Plan: step 6a tool evaluation outcome)

What was looked at

  • C++ source files in external/SimuCell3D/src/ (io/mesh_reader.cpp, solver.cpp, forces/).
  • MATLAB/Python source in external/ShElastic and external/Phase_field_Apoptosis.
  • Prototype L-BFGS-B triangulated cortical shell energy/gradient evaluation timings.

Observations

  1. SimuCell3D (external/SimuCell3D, Candidate A) rejected without C++ rewrite:
  2. Requires legacy ASCII VTK polyhedra (CELL_TYPES 42) with strict genus-0 manifold topology and runs Ball-Pivoting / edge-refinement that fails on concave or handle-containing masks.
  3. Pressure cell::pressure_ is uniform across the entire cell (<INMForce> is unimplemented in C++); face types reset to 0 at every time step (solver.cpp:124), preventing a 2x stiffer synaptic patch without modifying C++ source.
  4. Explicit dynamic integration requires 10^4–10^5 steps (~37.5–375 s per forward relaxation), which is too slow for multi-chamber parameter fitting.
  5. ShElastic and Phase_field_Apoptosis rejected:
  6. ShElastic assumes a single homogeneous spherical target interface; Phase_field_Apoptosis is a regular-grid phase-field solver that does not take segmented 3D surface meshes.
  7. Custom Python quasi-static triangulated cortical shell (scipy L-BFGS-B, Candidate B1) selected:
  8. Consumes marching_cubes + trimesh surface meshes directly, supports heterogeneous tensions ($T_{\text{target}}$, $T_{T,\text{back}}$, $T_{T,\text{syn}} = 2\,T_{T,\text{back}}$) and local synaptic pressure $p_{\text{act}}$, and relaxes in ~0.17–0.25 s per solve.

Decision

Selected the custom Python quasi-static two-shell cortical model (src/mech/shell_model.py) for Step 6b (commit 159df33). All subsequent Step 6b runs remain UNVERIFIED and parked at Gate G5.

Open issues

  • As later shown in 2026-10-28-02-step6b-runs-void and 2026-10-28-04-visual-review-2026-10-28, the two-shell solver lacked rigid-body pinning on the non-contact target hemisphere and exhibited path-dependent equilibria on real masks.
